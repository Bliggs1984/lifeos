How this system works, in fifteen minutes

Payoff: after this you know the four things the app does, the one thing it deliberately refuses to do, and where your assistant fits. You will also have three real items in your inbox, which is the whole system running.

01The shape of the screen

Top to bottom, the app is ordered by what matters at 7 a.m.:

02The one thing it refuses to do

the rule
Nothing pushes. No toast, no email, no red badge, no streak-shaming. The tab being open in the morning is the whole mechanism.

The original owner of this system had a morning brief emailed to him for nineteen days straight and read it zero times. Anything that can be blocked out will be blocked out. So the app gives you something worth looking at and then stays quiet.

why it mattersIf you have ADHD, or just a full life, a system that guilts you about gaps is a system you will abandon. Here missed days cost nothing. Streaks start a new run. A check-in after a fortnight away is a normal check-in.

03The overdue mechanic

A task that is past due leaves the list and appears once, near the top, with three moves:

take the 10-min version
Only offered when the task has a bite: a real ten-minute first move that your assistant wrote at curation. The task's title becomes the bite and the due date clears.
rescope
Flags the task so the next curation rewrites it. You are saying "this matters but it is the wrong shape".
let it go
Moves it to Someday. Someday is a parking lot, not a graveyard, and it never nags.

The reasoning: if something went overdue, you had already decided it was not important enough. Showing it again in red trains you to ignore the screen. The only useful response is to change the task until its importance is felt.

04The learning deck

Learning is organised in tracks. Each track has an artifact: a tangible thing that exists when the track is finished, such as a working script, a one-page document, a set-up machine. The dots under the card count sessions toward it.

Each morning the app deals the first queued card. Three buttons:

ButtonWhat happens
DoneAsks for one takeaway line, appends it to the ledger, ticks a habit whose name contains "learn" if you have one.
SwapDeals the next card. The card you skipped goes to the back and its swap count goes up. Two swaps means it was scoped wrong; curation shrinks it.
Not todayCard hides until tomorrow. No consequence.

05Where the assistant fits

The app is the glanceable surface. An AI assistant working in this folder is the review engine. It runs four rituals, described in CLAUDE.md:

RitualCadenceWhat it does
Check-inDaily, 5 to 10 minInbox to zero, pick today's three, tick habits, log a note.
CurateWeekly, 10 minRestock the learning queue, write lessons, rescope anything overdue.
Weekly reviewWeekly, 15 to 30 minShipped list first, then carried tasks, goals, patterns.
PortfolioQuarterly, 20 to 30 minWhere hours, importance and satisfaction sit across your life.

The assistant edits the JSON files directly. The app picks up its edits when the tab regains focus, or with the reload button.

06Do it

Go back to the app tab. Type three things that are in your head right now into the capture box, one at a time, Enter after each. Anything counts. Do not sort them.

That is the system running. Sorting happens at check-in, which is card 2.
